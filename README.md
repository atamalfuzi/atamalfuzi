# Hi, I'm Ata Malfuzi

Data scientist and economist in Vancouver, BC. I build data pipelines, econometric models and energy-economic systems that turn complex datasets into decisions for government, research and policy.

- **Energy-economic modelling:** transportation electrification, carbon markets, policy scenario analysis
- **Applied econometrics:** time series, state-space models, panel data, index construction
- **Stack:** R, Python, SQL, Julia, Azure, Git

## Work samples

Personal projects built from publicly available data. They are not associated with my work for any current or past employer and do not represent their views.

- [bc_electricity_balance](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/bc_electricity_balance): how rare BC's 2023 drought was, what it cost in hydro output and trade, how trade with the US moves hour by hour, and how well demand and trade can be forecast, from open data with [interactive charts](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/bc-electricity-balance.html) and a [monthly dashboard](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/bc_electricity_balance/) (Python, statsmodels, scikit-learn)
- [canada_gas_demand](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/canada_gas_demand): how households respond to natural gas prices since 1981, whether BC differs from other provinces, where the fall in use came from, and how well demand can be forecast, from open data with [interactive charts](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/canada-gas-demand.html) and a [monthly dashboard](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/canada_gas_demand/) (Python, statsmodels, scikit-learn)
- [bc_wildfire_smoke](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/bc_wildfire_smoke): how much of BC's PM2.5 is wildfire smoke since 2005, using a rule checked against BC's own list and satellite smoke maps, with trends, clusters of stations that share smoke, [interactive charts](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/bc-wildfire-smoke.html) and a [seasonal dashboard](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/bc_wildfire_smoke/) (Python, pandas, scikit-learn)
- [canada_minimum_wage](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/canada_minimum_wage): what 22 large minimum wage rises did to pay and jobs in Canada since 2006, where low-paid jobs go, whether jobs and hours fall, and how far raises reach up the pay scale, from public survey microdata with [interactive charts](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/canada-minimum-wage.html) and a [monthly dashboard](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/canada_minimum_wage/) (Python, pandas, scikit-learn)
- [canada_productivity_wages](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/canada_productivity_wages): [how far pay follows productivity](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/canada-productivity-wages.html) in 13 sectors and 10 provinces, 1997 to 2024, from open Statistics Canada tables and 8.1 million public survey records (Python, pandas, SciPy)
- [canada_indigenous_income_gap](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/canada_indigenous_income_gap): [how the income and wage gap](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/indigenous-income-gap.html) between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Canadians with the same observed characteristics changed across four census files, 2006 to 2021. It describes a gap and doesn't identify causes, and the people counted as Indigenous changed across the files (Python, pandas, NumPy)
- [boc_statements](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/boc_statements): how the Bank of Canada's language has changed since 2001 and when bond markets respond, from 207 rate statements with text methods and an event study, with an [interactive chart](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/bank-of-canada-statements.html) and a [statement explorer](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/boc_statements/) (Python, pandas, scikit-learn)
- [canada_business_cycles](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/canada_business_cycles): [how three filters read Canada's business cycles](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/canada-business-cycles.html) since 1961, and what each makes of the 2020 recession (Python, pandas, SciPy)
- [capm_factor_tests](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/capm_factor_tests): [tests of the CAPM and the Fama-French models](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/capm-factor-tests.html) on US stock returns through 2026, using GRS, Fama-MacBeth and Hansen-Jagannathan distance (Python, pandas, SciPy)
- [canada_incumbency_rd](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/canada_incumbency_rd): [whether narrowly winning a federal riding helps a party win it again](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/canada-incumbency.html), a replication of Kendall and Rekkas extended through 2025 with data-driven bandwidths and riding links across redistributions (Python, rdrobust)
- [btc_forecast_check](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/btc_forecast_check): a [corrected rebuild](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/bitcoin-forecast-check.html) of my 2022 MA paper on Bitcoin forecasting. Its headline score came from look-ahead, and in walk-forward tests no model beats a forecast of no change (Python, statsmodels, scikit-learn)
- [pow_welfare_cost](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/pow_welfare_cost): a [corrected re-analysis](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/proof-of-work-welfare-cost.html) of my 2021 MA thesis on the welfare cost of Proof of Work. Errors in the Ethereum inputs decided the thesis's answer, and on the same year Ethereum comes out somewhat lower than Bitcoin (Python, NumPy, DuckDB)
- [bc_pop_change_map](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/bc_pop_change_map): [maps](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/bc-population-change.html) of population change across British Columbia's census subdivisions (R, sf, ggplot2)
- [bc_housing_affordability](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/bc_housing_affordability): [rent and mortgage costs](https://atamalfuzi.github.io/rent-and-mortgage-burden.html) as a share of family income across BC's cities, from open data (R, ggplot2)
- [economics_coursework](https://github.com/atamalfuzi/economics_coursework): Stata and R from my MA at SFU, with replications of published papers using regression discontinuity, instrumental variables and difference-in-differences, CAPM tests, option pricing and business cycle filters, plus my BSc project comparing CNG and biogas in one engine (Stata, R)
